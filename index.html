4Position

The grid is the plan. The cats are what actually happens. This page is the space between them.

What is ordered

My life runs on a grid. Two kids, four cats, Ben, a design master's degree: none of it works without a timetable. School pickup is a column. Feeding time is a row. I bake by feel weight, not by guessing, and a Saint-Honoré is six hours of steps in exactly the right order. That part of me is Swiss. So the backbone of this page is Swiss too: twelve columns across the whole screen, rows of eighty pixels, and every thing on the page has its coordinates written down. Two typefaces that were already on every computer, Times for sentences and Helvetica for facts: I chose the defaults on purpose, which is not the same as accepting them. White, black, red for the grid, blue for my own hand.

What breaks it

Sofi knocks the plan off the table. Tova sleeps belly up in the middle of the schedule. The Saint-Honoré takes six hours and is gone in six minutes. Poppy seeds get into everything: the dough, the keyboard, this website. The photo of me at 7am and the one of me asleep are not decoration. They are the parts of my life that no grid has ever held and no model could have guessed. They don't sit next to the order. They land on it, in corners, at the edges, a few pixels out of line, because that is how it happens at home. On this page the distance is measured and written down.

Against templatology

Ask an AI for a personal site and you get Exhibit A: a centered hero, a purple gradient, three rounded cards, Inter, and a sentence about crafting delightful experiences. It looks organised, but nobody made a decision. The radius is 16px because everyone's is. The copy fits every designer, so it describes none. Müller-Brockmann's grid was a belief about clarity and honesty. The template is that belief with the belief taken out. So I put it on the page, tiny and grey, in a corner, next to the list of what I decided instead. The real order is an inventory: six things, numbered, placed, with a reason each.

The encounter

I don't think either side wins. A day where the plan survives untouched is perfect boring. A day with no plan is a disaster. My identity is the moment in between, when a thing is a few pixels off its cell and I can still see the cell underneath. That is why the grid can be switched on at any time on this page, and why the cats can be dialled down to zero but the page looks wrong without them. The rupture never erases the order. The order is what makes the rupture readable, and the rupture is what proves a person lives here.
