Wall text: position

The grid is the plan. The cats are what actually happens. This page is the space between them.

What is ordered

My life runs on a grid. Two kids, four cats, Ben, a design master's degree: none of it works without a timetable. School pickup is a column. Feeding time is a row. I bake by feel weight, not by guessing, and a Saint-Honoré is six hours of steps in exactly the right order. That part of me is Swiss. So the backbone of this page is Swiss too: twelve columns, a 24px gutter, an 8px baseline, one typeface in four sizes, black, white and one red. I chose every one of those numbers and wrote them in the margin of the card, so you can check me. You watch the grid draw itself before anything lands on it.

What breaks it

Sofi knocks the plan off the table. Tova sleeps belly up in the middle of the schedule. The Saint-Honoré takes six hours and is gone in six minutes. Poppy seeds get into everything: the dough, the keyboard, this website. The five rejected passport photos, the one at 7am, the one asleep, the one with the egg, are not decoration. They are the parts of my life that no grid has ever held and no model could have guessed. On this page they arrive one at a time, in the order they arrive at home, and each one pulls the card a little further off its columns. The distance is measured: the margin says how much order is left.

Against templatology

Ask an AI for a personal site and you get Exhibit A: a centered hero, a purple gradient, three rounded cards, Inter, and a sentence about crafting delightful experiences. It looks organised, but nobody made a decision. The radius is 16px because everyone's is. The copy fits every designer, so it describes none. Müller-Brockmann's grid was a belief about clarity and honesty. The template is that belief with the belief taken out. So it gets its own scene near the end, over the dimmed card, and a red line through it. Then the card comes back, with the cats on it.

The encounter

I don't think either side wins. A day where the plan survives untouched is perfect boring. A day with no plan is a disaster. My identity is the moment in between: the step where the order is restored and the cats stay exactly where they were. The rupture never erases the order. The order is what makes the rupture readable, and the rupture is what proves a person lives here. That is why the story ends with both on the screen, and why you can scroll back up and watch it happen again.
